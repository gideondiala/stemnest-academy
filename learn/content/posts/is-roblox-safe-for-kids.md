If your child has asked for Roblox, you have probably heard two very different stories. One says it is a creative playground that teaches children to build games. The other says it is full of strangers and hidden costs. Both have some truth in them, and a lot of the advice online is out of date, because Roblox changed its rules heavily in 2026.

This guide explains how Roblox works now, what the real risks are, and what to set up before your child starts playing.

> **The short answer:** Roblox is much more locked down for children than it was a year ago, and the controls are good if you actually use them. It is not risk-free, so it works best for younger children when a parent is linked to the account and talking about it regularly.

## What is Roblox?

Roblox is a free platform full of games made by its users. Your child does not play one game. They choose from a huge library made by other people, from obstacle courses to pet simulators. Some children also use the free Roblox Studio tool to [make their own games](/how-to-make-a-roblox-game-for-beginners/), which is where it becomes a real way to learn coding.

## What changed in 2026

Two big changes matter for parents.

**1. Accounts now depend on age.** In June 2026, Roblox rolled out age-based accounts worldwide. Exact ages and features can vary by country, so treat this table as the general picture:

| Account | Age | Games | Chat |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Roblox Kids** | 5 to 8 | Only Minimal or Mild ratings | Off, unless a parent allows in-game text chat |
| **Roblox Select** | 9 to 15 | Up to Moderate rating | Gradually increases with age, and parents can change it |
| **Roblox** | 16 and over | Standard account | On |

Games for Kids and Select accounts go through extra checks. By default, games focused on social hangouts, free-form drawing or sensitive issues are not included.

**2. Chat needs an age check.** Everyone must complete an age check to use chat, whatever their age. This is done by a face scan that estimates age, or by ID. A child who has not done the check cannot chat, and Roblox says they are also limited to games rated Minimal or Mild.

Chat is limited to people in the same or a similar age group, plus "Trusted Friends". Text filters apply to chat, and Roblox says all chat is monitored for child exploitation.

### Should my child do the age check?

You do not have to rush. If your child is young and does not need chat, skipping the check simply leaves chat off and keeps them on the mildest games. If you do use it, sit with them while they do it. Roblox says images are deleted after the check. Age estimates can be wrong, and parents can correct a child's age.

## The real risks

Better rules reduce the risks, but they do not remove them. These are the ones to talk about at home.

- **Spending.** Roblox is free, but it has its own currency called Robux, bought with real money. Many games are designed to encourage spending. Set a limit before your child asks.
- **People moving the chat elsewhere.** A stranger may try to move a conversation to another app, where Roblox's filters do not apply. Teach your child that this is a red flag, every time.
- **Scams.** Offers of "free Robux" are always fake. They are used to steal accounts or passwords.
- **Time.** Games are built to bring players back. A daily limit agreed in advance saves many arguments later.
- **Upsetting content or behaviour.** Ratings and filters help, but no system catches everything. Your child needs to know they can tell you without getting into trouble.

## What parents can control

To use the parental controls, you need your own Roblox account linked to your child's. Once linked, this is what you can do:

| Until your child turns | You can |
|---|---|
| **13** | See their friends list, block or report users, approve or decline Trusted Friend requests, and set screen-time and spending limits |
| **16** | Change chat settings and allow or block specific games |

From 13, your child takes over some of those choices, which is a good reason to build the habits early. Roblox also lets parents of 9 to 12 year olds lower the highest game rating their child can reach.

## A 10-minute setup checklist

Do this together, so it feels like teamwork and not like a punishment.

1. Create the account with your child's **real date of birth**. This decides which account they get.
2. Use a username that does not include their real name, school or birth year.
3. Create your own account and **link it** to your child's.
4. Decide whether chat is needed at all. For under 9s, the simple answer is usually no.
5. Set a **spending limit**, even if it is zero.
6. Set a **daily screen-time limit**.
7. For a child aged 9 to 12, check the game rating limit and lower it if you want to.
8. Turn on two-step verification, so a stolen password is not enough to take the account.

## Five rules to agree with your child

- Never share your real name, school, address, phone number or passwords.
- Never move a chat to another app because someone asked you to.
- Nobody gives away free Robux. If it sounds too good to be true, it is a trick.
- Never spend money without asking first.
- If something feels strange or upsetting, tell me. You will not be in trouble.

## Frequently asked questions

**What age is Roblox suitable for?** Roblox has accounts for children from age 5, but that does not mean every 5 year old should play alone. Under 9s get the most limited account and do best with a parent nearby.

**Can strangers talk to my child on Roblox?** A child who has not done an age check cannot use chat at all. After an age check, chat is limited to similar age groups and Trusted Friends, and parents can turn chat off for under 13s.

**Can I stop my child spending money?** Yes. Until your child turns 13, you can set spending limits through parental controls. Also check that no bank card is saved on the device.

**Does my child need a face scan?** Only if they need chat. Without an age check, they can still play, but with no chat and only games rated Minimal or Mild.

**Do these rules apply in my country?** Roblox says exact ages, games and features vary by region, and its rules keep changing. Check the [Roblox Safety Center](https://about.roblox.com/safety) for the latest details where you live.
