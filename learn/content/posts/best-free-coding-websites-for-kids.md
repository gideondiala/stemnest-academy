You don't need to spend money to start your child on coding. Some of the best learning tools in the world are completely free, made by universities, charities and big technology companies that want more young people to learn.

The tricky part is knowing which ones are worth your child's time. Here's our pick of the best free coding websites and apps, grouped by age, with what each is good for and how to begin.

> Prices and features can change, so check each site's current terms. Everything listed here offered free access to its core learning tools at the time of writing.

## Ages 5 to 7

### ScratchJr

**What it is:** a free tablet app from the creators of Scratch, where young children snap together picture blocks to make characters move, jump and talk.
**Why we like it:** almost no reading needed, and children make their own stories and simple games from the very first session.
**Get started:** download ScratchJr from your tablet's app store. Sit with your child for the first few sessions.

### Code.org (early courses)

**What it is:** a non-profit offering free coding courses for every age, including picture-based puzzles for young children.
**Why we like it:** short, structured activities with friendly characters, and a clear sense of progress.
**Get started:** visit code.org and choose a course for your child's age.

## Ages 7 to 12

### Scratch

**What it is:** the world's most popular coding platform for children, made by the MIT Media Lab. Children build games, animations and stories with colourful blocks.
**Why we like it:** it teaches every core programming idea without typing frustrations, and it's genuinely creative. Children can share projects and look inside other people's code to learn.
**Get started:** go to scratch.mit.edu and click **Create**. Our step-by-step [catch game tutorial](/how-to-make-a-catch-game-in-scratch/) is a perfect first project, and our list of [25 Scratch project ideas](/scratch-project-ideas-for-beginners/) will keep them busy for months.

### Blockly Games

**What it is:** a free set of puzzle games from Google that teach programming concepts with blocks, gradually moving towards typed JavaScript.
**Why we like it:** the later levels show the real code behind the blocks, which makes it a natural bridge to text coding.
**Get started:** search for Blockly Games and start with the first puzzle.

### Teachable Machine

**What it is:** a free Google tool for training simple AI models with your webcam, no coding required.
**Why we like it:** it makes artificial intelligence hands-on and understandable, and it sparks great conversations about how AI learns.
**Get started:** follow our guide to [training your first AI model](/train-an-ai-model-with-teachable-machine/).

## Ages 10 and up

### MIT App Inventor

**What it is:** a free tool from MIT for building real Android apps with blocks.
**Why we like it:** children can make an app and run it on a real phone within an hour, which is hugely motivating.
**Get started:** our guide to [making your first app with App Inventor](/make-an-app-with-mit-app-inventor/) walks through two complete apps.

### Python with Thonny

**What it is:** Python is a free, professional programming language, and Thonny is a free editor designed especially for beginners.
**Why we like it:** it's the most common next step after Scratch, and it opens the door to games, data and AI.
**Get started:** install Thonny from thonny.org (it includes Python), then try our [Python number guessing game](/python-number-guessing-game-for-kids/), [10 Python projects](/python-projects-for-beginners/) or [turtle drawing](/python-turtle-drawing-for-kids/).

### Khan Academy (computing)

**What it is:** a non-profit learning site with free computer programming lessons, including drawing and animation with JavaScript.
**Why we like it:** interactive lessons where you change code and see the result instantly.
**Get started:** visit khanacademy.org and look for the computing section.

### Swift Playgrounds

**What it is:** Apple's free app for learning the Swift language through puzzles and challenges, on iPad and Mac.
**Why we like it:** beautifully designed, and a real step towards making iPhone apps.
**Get started:** download Swift Playgrounds from the App Store.

## Ages 12 and up

### Building websites: HTML, CSS and JavaScript

**What it is:** the three languages of the web. All you need is a free code editor like VS Code and a browser, or a free online editor such as CodePen.
**Why we like it:** teenagers can build and publish real websites they can share with friends.
**Get started:** read [HTML vs CSS vs JavaScript explained](/html-css-javascript-explained/), then build [your first website](/build-your-first-website-html-css/).

### freeCodeCamp

**What it is:** a non-profit with free, self-paced courses in web development, JavaScript, Python and more.
**Why we like it:** a huge amount of structured material and projects, suited to motivated older teens.
**Get started:** visit freecodecamp.org. It's text-heavy, so it works best for independent learners.

### Roblox Studio

**What it is:** the free tool used to make Roblox games, programmed with a language called Lua.
**Why we like it:** for children who love Roblox, building their own game is incredibly motivating.
**Get started:** follow our guide to [making your first Roblox game](/how-to-make-a-roblox-game-for-beginners/). Set it up together with a parent and review Roblox's parental controls.

## How to choose

With so many options, it's easy to jump from site to site. Try this approach instead:

1. **Pick one tool that matches your child's age** using the sections above.
2. **Stick with it for a few weeks**, building complete projects.
3. **Move up when they're ready.** Signs include finishing projects easily and asking for things the tool can't do. Our guide to [Scratch vs Python](/scratch-vs-python-which-first/) explains the most common switch.

## When free isn't quite enough

Free tools are fantastic, but they have a limit: there's no one to help when your child gets stuck, and no plan to follow from beginner to advanced. Many children make great progress alone; others lose motivation without guidance. If your child is in the second group, a structured class with a live tutor can make all the difference. Our [parents' guide to teaching kids to code](/how-to-teach-kids-to-code-parents-guide/) covers how to support them either way.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the best free coding website for kids?** For most children aged 7 to 12, Scratch is the best place to start. For younger children, try ScratchJr. For teenagers, Python with Thonny or web development with HTML and CSS.

**Are free coding websites safe for children?** Most educational platforms have safety rules and moderation, but it's always wise to supervise younger children, check privacy settings, and talk about never sharing personal information online.

**Can my child really learn coding for free?** Yes. Children can learn a great deal with free tools. Many families add a class or tutor later for structure, feedback and help when things get difficult.

**Do kids need a powerful computer to learn coding?** No. Most of these tools run in a web browser on an ordinary laptop or Chromebook, and some work on tablets.
